html-revisor: reply design

The agent answers in the page. Each element carries its own small conversation, and many run at once while you keep reviewing.

Decided

question answer
Where do replies live? In the page's HTML file, beside the directive. They commit with the turn, and cleanup removes them.
Who writes a reply into the file? The server. The agent hands it the reply through a CLI that calls the running server, so the server orders the agent's writes with the browser's saves.
What if the directive changes while the agent replies? The server refuses the agent's write as stale, the same way it refuses a browser save on an element that changed.
How does the tab learn of a reply? The server tells it, over the live notice: the server watches the file and pushes to the open tab.
Where does a reply show? In the directive box, below the directive. Each element has its own thread.
How do you know the agent has it? A directive carries a status: working, then answered. The element's marker shows it.
What does the docket count? Both: N directives · M replies.
What can get a reply? A directive, or an edit: “I also changed 3 other places that used the old word.” “Want me to update the other documents to match?”

Flow

One directive, start to answer. The Announcement on stdout already exists; the rest is new. Magenta arrows are the live notice.

Directive box with a thread

Until the agent takes it, the box is today's: Content, Directive, Done. Once the agent says working, the directive moves into the thread as your first turn, and the field below the thread is for your answer. You read top to bottom and type at the bottom. A rule at the left of each turn says whose it is: magenta yours, green the agent's. The words "you" and "agent" say it too. Your turns are monospace, since each is a directive; the agent's are prose. The thread grows to 240px, then scrolls, and opens scrolled to the end.

A: one directive, rewritten. The reply sits under the directive. To answer, you rewrite the directive, and the next reply replaces this one. The file holds one directive and one reply, but the history is gone, and the field you type in sits above what you're answering.

Why B. You answer where you read, and the agent's next turn has the whole exchange in front of it. A asks you to fold your answer into the directive, which loses what the agent said and why.

What B costs. The directive can't be edited once the agent has it; you add a turn instead. That also settles the stale case for the directive: it stops changing under the agent. A turn you add while the agent works is the new stale case.

A reply on an edit. You changed Content and left no directive. The thread opens with the edit as your turn: what changed, old word struck. The agent answers that.

Marker: working and answered

The tile stays: near-black, light ring, square. What's on it changes shape with the state, and the color says whose move it was: magenta yours, green the agent's.

  • Waiting: today's magenta pencil. Your directive, not taken yet.
  • Working: three green squares, pulsing. The agent has it. With reduced motion, they hold still.
  • Answered: a green speech bubble, filled. Your turn.
  • Read: the bubble, hollow. You opened the reply and left it without answering.

What it shows. Green alone fails. Under deuteranopia, the most common kind, the magenta turns khaki (#878168) and the green turns a lighter khaki (#c2b36e): the two pencils differ in lightness alone, and a 14px glyph beside busy page text doesn't hold that difference at a glance. Tritanopia keeps them apart (red against teal), protanopia by lightness. So the shape carries the state and the color backs it up. This green, #51cf66, kept the most distance from the magenta of five greens under protanopia and deuteranopia, and it is lighter than the magenta, which helps in greyscale.

Recommended The bubble. It changes the whole glyph, so it reads at 14px in any vision, and a speech bubble says "a reply" without a legend. The badge works too, but an 8px square at the tile's corner gets lost beside a neighbor marker, and the pencil under it still says "your directive". The green pencil fails the check above.

Docket

Each open thread counts once. Directives are the ones on the agent's move: waiting or working. Replies are the ones on yours: answered. The two add up to every open thread on the page. Stepping to a reply works as stepping to a directive does today: the element scrolls to the middle, shows its crop marks, and its marker shows its tip, which for a reply is the reply's start.

Open questions

  • What does a reply look like in the file? A second comment kind beside the directive? It stays out of the element's content hash, as a directive does.
  • You answer a reply: does that edit the directive, or add to the thread?
  • When is a thread done, and who closes it? Today the agent deletes a directive once it's addressed.
  • “Reply”, “working”, “answered” are words you see: a Contract change, carried to iterate-on-docs.
  • A second color. DESIGN.html says color means one thing, the accent. These prototypes add green for the agent's voice: magenta is yours, green is the agent's. Is that a rule you want?
  • Once the agent has a directive, can you still edit it? The thread (#box, B) says no: you add a turn. Then the stale case moves to a turn added while the agent works. Does the agent see that turn before it replies, or after?
  • Is “read” a state worth drawing? If opening the box doesn't change the count, the hollow bubble is the only thing that knows you've seen it.
  • Resolve, in the box, is a new word you see. Who ends a thread: you, the agent, or either?
  • Should the agent offer yes and no as buttons when it asks a yes-or-no question?
  • The keyboard: with two steppers (#docket, B), which do ⌥↑ and ⌥↓ step? The prototype gives them to replies, the threads on your move.
  • A reply that names other places (“I also changed 3 other places”) links to them. How does the agent name an element so the box can step to it: by its element ID?
